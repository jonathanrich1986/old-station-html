Our Story

From working railway station to beloved community destination.

Est. Circa 1860

A Victorian Legacy

The Old Station in Welshpool was built in the 1860s as part of the historic Cambrian Railways network, serving as a vital transport hub for the market town of Welshpool and the surrounding rural communities of mid-Wales.

For over a century, the station welcomed passengers, transported agricultural goods, and formed the beating heart of commerce in Powys. Its distinctive Victorian architecture — red brick, ornate ironwork, wide canopied platforms — stands as a testament to the ambition of the railway age.

When passenger services were relocated, the original station building faced an uncertain future. Rather than see this treasured piece of heritage fall into disrepair, a passionate group of local investors and preservationists came together with a vision: to breathe new life into the station while honouring everything that made it special.

Through the Years

Our Timeline

1860s

Station Opens

Welshpool station opens as part of the Cambrian Railways network, transforming trade and travel across Powys.

1900s

Golden Age of Rail

The station becomes one of the busiest in the region, serving hundreds of passengers and freight trains daily through both World Wars.

1990s

Closure & New Vision

Following the closure of the mainline station, the historic building begins its transformation, with restoration work commencing to preserve its Victorian character.

Today

A Destination Reborn

The Old Station Welshpool is now a thriving retail and café destination, home to independent shops, artisan food and a welcoming community atmosphere.
